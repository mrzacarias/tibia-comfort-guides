# Sorcerer hunting guide by comfort (Tibia, Nov 2026)

This guide is for anyone playing a sorcerer alone, including players who are brand new to Tibia. It answers one question at every level: **where can I hunt without stress, and still make good experience and gold?**

Everything here describes Tibia as it is in November 2026, after the big vocation changes of June 2026. Older guides and videos may give different advice, because spells, damage and potions changed a lot.

**The numbers in this guide are untested estimates.** They come from the same model as the knight guide, changed for a sorcerer's HP, spells and way of fighting. Real hunts will correct them over time.

## How to use this guide
The game has many systems that make a sorcerer stronger: imbuements, charms, the Wheel of Destiny and more. Each one helps, but each one is also something new to learn, buy or keep track of. This guide adds them **one at a time**, in sections:

- **Section 1, Basic mechanics:** just your wand, two attack spells, one healing spell and potions. Full hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1a, Invisibility:** a spell that hides you from many monsters. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1b, Hunting grounds behind quests:** good hunting grounds that open only after part of a quest, and the quests to do. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1c, Hunting grounds with a long trip:** good hunting grounds more than about 2 minutes from the depot. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 2, Imbuements:** life and mana leech on your gear. Full hunting ground lists.
- **Section 3, Charms and prey:** bonuses against the monsters you hunt. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 4, Wheel of Destiny and gems:** permanent perks from your level. Hunting ground lists.
- **Sections 5 and 6, Weapon proficiency and the Forge:** the last steps to a full setup.

Start in Section 1. Find your level in the hunting ground tables and pick a hunting ground from its **Good for XP** list to level up, or from its **Good for gold** list when you need money for gear, supplies or your promotion. When hunting there feels easy, move on to the next section and see which new hunting grounds open up.

If you test a hunting ground, write down how it went in `spot_tests.md`. Tested hunting grounds will be marked with ✓ and use real numbers instead of estimates.

## Words you'll see
- **HP and mana:** your health and the energy you spend on spells. A sorcerer has little HP, about 185 + 5 × (level − 8): 395 at level 50, 815 at level 134. A knight has about three times as much. Mana is the opposite: a sorcerer gets 30 mana per level.
- **Magic level:** how strong your spells are. It grows as you spend mana.
- **Spawn:** the monsters that live in a hunting ground and come back after you kill them.
- **Kiting:** attacking from a distance while walking away, so monsters that hit up close can't reach you.
- **Wand:** a sorcerer's weapon. It attacks your target from a few squares away.
- **Strike:** a spell that hits one monster (`exori flam`, `exori vis`, `exori mort`).
- **Wave:** a spell that hits every monster in a cone in front of you (`exevo flam hur`, later `exevo vis hur`).
- **`exura`, `exura gran`:** the basic healing spells.
- **XP/h, loot/h:** experience and gold (the value of what monsters drop) per hour of hunting.
- **Depot:** your storage in each city. It's also where the Market is.
- **Market:** where players buy and sell items. Many monster drops sell there for far more than shops pay.
- **Band:** a level range in the hunting ground tables, such as 40–60: 20 levels apart up to 100, then 50.

## Comfort: the idea behind this guide
Some hunting grounds ask almost nothing of you: you walk in, attack, and never need to heal. Others ask a lot: you have to watch your health all the time, carry many potions, avoid certain rooms, or bring special gear. This guide calls that difference **comfort**.

Comfort depends on your level. A hunting ground that is dangerous at level 50 can be relaxing at level 130, because you have more HP and hit harder.

Every hunting ground gets two grades from **0 (most comfortable) to 3 (least comfortable)**:
- **Hunt:** how much attention the hunt itself needs.
- **Prep:** how much you need to bring or buy before you go.

| Grade | Hunt (during the hunt) | Prep (before the hunt) |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | You attack and keep your distance. No healing needed. | Only the mana potions every sorcerer brings. |
| **1** | You heal now and then. | A few extra potions. |
| **2** | You heal often and must keep moving so monsters can't catch you. | Many potions, or a specific piece of gear. |
| **3** | You need special tactics: invisible monsters, monsters that call helpers, rooms to avoid. | Something from a later section of this guide. |

The best hunting grounds are **Prep 0, Hunt 0** ("P0 H0" in short). That's the dream: no worries, just hunting.

Every sorcerer drinks mana potions to cast spells, so those don't count against Prep. Their cost is counted, though: a hunting ground is only listed if its loot pays for them.

There's a third kind of comfort, **knowledge**: how much of the game you need to understand. That's what the sections are for. Each section has a **kit**, the things everyone in that section uses. In Section 2, for example, everyone has the two leech imbuements, so they don't count against a hunting ground's Prep grade.

### How a hunting ground is graded
Until hunting grounds are tested with a sorcerer, every grade comes from the fight-by-fight model in the appendix. It checks two things for the band's typical sorcerer:
- **How much you need to heal:** the HP the monsters take from you in an hour, after your armor, your spellbook and your protections, counted in full HP bars.
- **The worst moment:** the hardest round of a typical pull, every monster in it hitting its hardest at once, as a share of your HP.

| Healing per hour | Worst round | Hunt and Prep |
|---|---|---|
| less than 3 HP bars | less than 30% of your HP | 0 |
| less than 15 | less than 50% | 1 |
| less than 40 | less than 80% | 2 |
| more | more | 3 |

The grade is the first row where both numbers fit. For example, a hunting ground where you heal 10 full HP bars an hour and the worst round takes 40% of your HP is Hunt 1.

**Kiting.** A sorcerer fights from a distance, so a monster's melee hits count only half: you'll still get caught now and then, in corners or when a group surrounds you. Spells and thrown weapons reach you anyway, so they count in full. This is why monsters that cast spells or throw spears matter much more to a sorcerer than to a knight. With so little HP, the same hunting ground often grades higher than in the knight guide.

A few things still raise it:
- **Invisible monsters.** You can't see them to attack them, so the hunting ground is Hunt 3.
- **A rare monster that can take half your HP in one turn** makes the hunting ground at least Hunt 2.
- **Special gear.** If the hunting ground needs a specific item, like an elemental armor or a Dwarven Ring, Prep is 2.

Monsters that call helpers are part of the model: the helpers' damage counts in your healing.

Some hunting grounds are left out entirely: those full of summoners, or with many monsters that hurt you but give no experience.

### Which hunting grounds are listed
A hunting ground shows up only from the level TibiaWiki suggests for it (the Brazilian TibiaWiki when it has the page), then in every band where our model rates it comfortable, so the same hunting ground often appears in several bands, and its grades and numbers get better as your level goes up.

Each band shows two short lists: **Good for XP**, the 3 hunting grounds with the most experience per hour, and **Good for gold**, the 3 with the most loot per hour. Hunting grounds whose loot doesn't cover the potions they cost are left out. Both lists take only comfortable hunting grounds (Prep and Hunt of 0 or 1), and allow a 2 only when fewer than 3 qualify. A hunting ground can be in both lists. The later sections show a band only when their system changes these lists. Hunting grounds that need a quest first are left out of every section except Section 1b (the Targuna Quest and starting The New Frontier Quest don't count), and hunting grounds more than about 2 minutes from the depot are only in Section 1c. The tables start at level 20, and Sections 2 to 4 at level 50.

### The character the numbers assume
The XP and loot numbers are estimates for a typical sorcerer: someone whose magic level is a little below the usual for their level (10 at level 8, 25 at 30, 58 at 80, 80 at 134, 95 at 200), using the best wand an NPC sells for that level. They wear the best armor they could buy with about 3 hours of hunting income. Each band shows that magic level and wand. If yours are better, you'll do better than the tables.

The estimates also assume that you wave any group of 2 or more (`exevo flam hur` hits up to 4 monsters, `exevo vis hur` up to 6) with strikes in between, and strike single monsters, always with the element each monster is weakest to.

## Section 1: Basic mechanics
In this section you use only the basics: your wand, two attack spells (a strike for one monster and a wave for groups), the `exura` healing spells, potions and normal gear. Everything else comes later.

### How to fight
**Keep your distance.** With so little HP, a sorcerer survives by not letting monsters reach them. This is **kiting**: attack from a few squares away and keep walking, so the monsters that hit up close never catch you. Never let a group surround you, and avoid corners and narrow corridors.

**Your attacks:** your wand shoots your target by itself every 2 seconds, and gives back a little mana. **Strikes** hit one monster up to 3 squares away for 20 mana: `exori flam` (fire, level 8), `exori vis` (energy, 12) and `exori mort` (death, 16); use the element the monster is weakest to. **Waves** hit a cone in front of you: `exevo flam hur` (fire, level 18), and from level 38 the much stronger `exevo vis hur` (energy, once every 8 seconds). Let the monsters follow you so they bunch up, then wave into the group. In the Magical Archive, switch on each wave's aiming mode so it fires at your target. Attack spells share a 2-second cooldown: wave, strike, wave, strike.

There's no spell that hits all around you here: with much less HP than a knight, standing in a group is the quickest way to die. **Elements matter**, too: dragons, for example, take no fire damage. The tables count each monster's weaknesses.

**Healing:** `exura` (level 8) and `exura gran` (level 20). Heal early: one big hit can take half your health. Since June 2026 the **Mana Buffer** saves you once from a killing hit by draining your mana instead; leave right away when it happens.

**Potions** share one short cooldown (about 1 second). Mana Potions are the cheapest mana (56 gold for about 100), and the tables count them as supplies.

**Stay safe:** always know your way out, leave when your HP falls to about half, and take extra care where the notes say "slows".

### Gear
In Section 1 you wear only items that never run out, so there are no charges or timers to watch. Each band's gear line lists good-value weapons and armor.
- **Wand:** buy the best one your level allows. They're cheap, and having one of each element helps against monsters that resist one:

- **Shield hand:** a spellbook. Since June 2026 spellbooks protect much more than before.
- **Amulet and ring:** a Platinum Amulet, then a Foxtail Amulet from level 100 (5% less physical damage), and a Butterfly Ring from level 50.
- **Armor:** armor, legs and helmets that add magic level are worth it; otherwise wear whatever protects most.

### Before you hunt
- **Stamina:** with a premium account, the first 3 hours from full stamina (42 hours) give 50% more experience, so start your best hunts after a long rest. Below 14 hours you get half the experience and no loot. Stamina comes back while you're logged out, but only after 10 minutes offline, so each quick login between hunts (to check the Market, for example) costs up to 10 minutes of refill, almost 2 minutes of green stamina.
- **Blessings:** the 5 regular blessings, bought from NPCs in the cities, make you lose about 70% less experience and skill when you die, and you keep your items. They cost about (level − 20) × 1,000 gold up to level 120. They're worth it: where and how in the knight guide's [Blessings, step by step](../knight/guide.md#blessings-step-by-step).
- **Food:** eat until the game says you're full before the hunt, and again now and then; food makes you regenerate.
- **Daily reward:** take it every day at the Reward Wall (potions or runes, Prey Wildcards, training weapons, an XP boost). The streak also makes you regenerate faster in temples, depots and houses.
- **Buy potions on the Market first:** potions handed out as daily rewards often sell there far below NPC prices.

### Loot
- **Quick Loot:** Shift + right click on a corpse loots it. In the Options, under Hotkeys, put **Quick Loot Nearby Corpses** on a key next to your attack keys: it loots your square and the 8 around it at once.
- **Skip the junk:** right-click an item and choose "Add to Loot List". In Skipped Loot mode, Quick Loot takes everything except those items.
- **Sort it:** right-click a backpack and choose "Manage Containers" to send each category (gold, creature products, potions and more) to its own backpack. Put a few empty backpacks inside your loot backpack and you can loot much longer.
- **Sell on the Market:** many drops sell there for far more than shops pay (a Stone Wing sells for about 2.4k on the Market and 120 at a shop); the notes name each hunting ground's best drop. Selling to a buy offer is instant, but check that it beats the shop price. At the depot, "Stow container's content" puts stackable loot in your Supply Stash, and the Market sells straight from there.
- **Skinning:** use an **Obsidian Knife** (about 10–20k on the Market, or the [Obsidian Knife Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Obsidian_Knife_Quest)) on Dragon corpses: about 1 in 24 gives a **Green Dragon Leather** (about 15k on the Market, November 2026), around 670 gold per dragon on average, which nearly triples a dragon's loot. Dragon Lords give a Red Dragon Leather (about 2k; around 170 gold per kill) and Behemoths a Perfect Behemoth Fang (about 3k; around 300). Minotaurs, lizards and bonebeasts give almost nothing. The Scavenge minor charm (Section 3) raises the chance.
- **Count the gold you actually get.** The Hunt Analyser can be far off in either direction, and prices differ between worlds, sometimes by five times, so check your own Market before you pick a hunting ground for its loot. You can set your own price for any item in the analyser: give items that players want, like the ones for weekly delivery tasks, their Market price.

### Bonuses on top
The tables don't count any of these or the Task Board below, so they all come on top.
- **The boosted creature:** every day at server save, one creature starts giving twice the experience and loot, and respawns twice as fast. It's shown on the login screen. If it lives in a hunting ground for your level, hunt there that day.
- **Echo Raids:** now and then a creature leaves an echo behind. Setting it off brings 8 more of that creature, or 4 influenced ones (stronger, and they give Dust), for 10 minutes. About 1 time in 10 (1 in 5 once that creature's Bestiary entry is finished) an **Echo Warden** comes instead: a stronger version that also makes the creatures near it stronger. A Warden always drops a Basic imbuement scroll and a weapon proficiency catalyst, and the first Warden of each creature gives 1 to 30 charm points, more for tougher creatures. Stepping on the echo sets it off (so does using it), so on a hunting ground that already keeps you busy, walk around it.
- **Discovery:** the Cyclopedia map splits Tibia into areas, each with points of interest to find. Find all of them in an area and you get 100 to 195 extra speed there for good, which makes walking between pulls and getting away easier. It's worth doing around the hunting grounds you use most.
- **Animus Mastery:** fiendish monsters (rare, at most 4 on a world at a time; `exiva moe res` points the way to one) drop a Soul Core about 1 time in 6, and Soul Cores also sell on the Market. Use one at the Soulpit, north-east of Hrodmir, and survive the waves of that creature it starts: from then on, that creature gives 2% more experience (up to 4% as you unlock more creatures). The waves don't get easier when you go alone, and dying there costs the usual, so it's optional.
- **Loyalty:** each 360 days of premium time the account has used add 5% to your skills, a small boost (about one level at skill 100), up to 50%. It only comes with time.

### Task Board: bounty and weekly tasks
The Task Board pays at every level, from your first hunts.

**Buy a Bounty Talisman as soon as you have 5,000 gold.** Jewellers in the cities sell it, and it goes in the extra slot. Even with no upgrades it gives **2.5% more damage, 2.5% life leech and 2.5% more loot** against your current bounty creature, and a 5% chance that a kill counts twice in the Bestiary. It works only while you wear it, and only against that creature. Its upgrades belong to your character, not to the item.

**Bounty tasks:** choose a difficulty (the same setting picks your weekly kill tasks) and the board offers 3 creatures. Take one, kill that many (summons' kills don't count) and report the task for a little experience, Bounty Points and a Reroll Token, which swaps the 3 offers for new ones. Now and then a task comes as Silver (twice the experience and points) or Gold (four times).

| Difficulty | Creatures (Bestiary difficulty) | Kills | Bounty Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Easy | 50–100 | 3 |
| Adept | Easy and Medium | 100–200 | 7 |
| Expert | Medium and Hard | 200–400 | 16 |
| Master | Hard and Challenging | 300–600 | 27 |

- **Take a creature from the hunting ground you're going to anyway.** The task then finishes by itself and the talisman works the whole hunt. If none of the 3 offers fits, use a Reroll Token. The Bestiary shows each creature's difficulty: Cyclopes and Minotaurs are Easy, while Stonerefiners, Dragons and Wyverns are Medium, so they come only from Adept up.
- **Preferred creatures:** mark your hunting ground's main creature as preferred and it shows up more often. The first slot is free; the next ones cost 300, 600, 900 and 1,200 points.
- **Spend the points on the talisman.** A bonus's first upgrade costs 5 points and each next one 12 more (5, 17, 29, 41 and so on), for 0.5% each (1% for the Bestiary chance). Reaching 5% costs 145 points in total, and 10% costs 1,335. Past 10% each upgrade adds less: 0.25%, then 0.1% from 20%. Start with damage, which speeds up every kill, then life leech.

**Weekly tasks:** 6 kill and 6 delivery tasks every Monday, drawn for you. Each gives half a level of experience up to level 82, and 1,994 × your level after that (about 183k at level 92). One kill task is "Any Creature", 1,000 kills of anything: it completes itself wherever you hunt. Deliveries ask for items you can buy on the Market, so they turn gold into experience: each one saves about 20 minutes of hunting at level 50 and 60 minutes at level 90. On your main, buy them when you have gold to spare. On a character you plan to sell, buy only the cheap ones (the [0 to 100 guide](zero_to_100.md) shows the limit by level). Prices differ a lot between worlds: 20 Silver Poniards cost 74k on one and 256k on another.

### Train your magic level while you're offline
Your magic level decides how hard your spells hit. Use a **Training Statue** (in every big city's training school, and on Targuna; later, a bed in your house), pick **Magic Level + Shielding** and log out. Training starts after 10 minutes and runs up to 12 hours; the time comes back as you play. A new sorcerer gains about 7 or 8 magic levels in the first 12 hours (roughly from 2 to 9 or 10). Hunting trains it much faster, because you spend a lot of mana. It's free, so do it every time you log out, from level 8.

**Faster, for gold:** an **exercise wand** used on an exercise dummy (every training school has them) trains 300,000 mana of magic level in 17 minutes, about two weeks of offline training. Each costs about 347k gold from magic shop NPCs, or 25 TC in the Store, so leave them for when gold comes easily. The daily reward's training weapons are small free ones.

### Levels 8 to 20: Targuna
The tables start at level 20, because the first levels are best spent on **Targuna**, the island for levels 8 to 20: its quests are safe, its depot is a few seconds from its hunting grounds, and its monsters drop potions you'll use later. Train offline for 12 hours first, then do the three missions of the [Targuna Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Targuna_Quest). With the hunting you do along the way, they take you to about level 20.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| [An ancient enemy](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Targuna_Quest/Spoiler#An_ancient_enemy) | Lizzie | any | Kill the Lizard Commander two floors down in the Hidden Lizard Temple | 1,500 experience, 2 platinum coins |
| [A long lost treasure](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Targuna_Quest/Spoiler#A_long_lost_treasure) | Sterling | 10 | On Aragonia, kill 20 pirates and use 10 tortoise eggs; bring Morla's map to Sterling, then dig at the marked place (bring a rope and a shovel) | 3,000 experience and an Adventurer Backpack |
| [Burning Heat](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Targuna_Quest/Spoiler#Burning_Heat) | Emiliana | 12 | Go down through the Crimson Court and beat the Herald of Fire | 5,000 experience and the Reliable Ram mount (give the turnips to the ram) |

Targuna's Hidden Lizard Temple and Aragonia come back in the 30–70 lists, so you can return later. Before you leave Newhaven, the starting island, do the [Newhaven Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Newhaven_Quest) (1,600 experience and a Guardian Ring) and the [Newhaven Legion Helmet Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Newhaven_Legion_Helmet_Quest); you can't go back. On the mainland, [The Scatterbrained Sorcerer Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/The_Scatterbrained_Sorcerer_Quest) (starts in Thais, 10,650 experience) is the best early one.

### Promotion at level 20
At **level 20**, with a premium account, buy your **promotion** for 20,000 gold to become a **Master Sorcerer**. The easiest place is King Tibianus III in Thais's castle; the rulers of Carlin, Kazordoon, Ankrahmun and Beregar and Rotem Valos at the Adventurers' Guild sell it too. Buy it as soon as you have the gold: you lose 30% less experience and skill when you die, regenerate faster, can learn promotion-only spells, and from level 51 get Wheel of Destiny points (Section 4). It pauses while your premium time is over.

### Spells worth learning later
The tables use only the basics, but a few spells make hunting calmer once you're used to them:
- **Magic Shield** (`utamo vita`, level 14, 50 mana) is the most important one. For 3 minutes, damage hits your mana instead of your HP, up to 7 × magic level + 7.6 × level + 300: about 960 at level 50 with magic level 40, more than twice your HP. It costs mana potions, so recast it as soon as it breaks.
- **Runes:** Great Fireball (level 30) and Thunderstorm (level 28) hit a big circle wherever you aim, good for groups you can't line up for a wave; Sudden Death (level 45) hits one monster hard. Every rune you use costs gold.
- **Big area spells** (premium): `exevo gran mas vis` (level 55) and `exevo gran mas flam` (level 60) hit a huge area around you once every 40 seconds, and `exevo max mort` (level 66) hits a long line.
- **`utani hur`** (level 14) makes you 30% faster, your best kiting tool, and a **Magic Wall** rune (level 32) blocks a corridor for about 20 seconds.
- **Aura of Sapped Strength** (stance, level 175): every monster you hit deals 10% less damage.
- **Emergency items:** keep a **Stone Skin Amulet** (80% less physical and death damage for 5 hits) or a **Might Ring** (20% less damage for 20 hits) in your backpack, and put it on when a pull goes wrong. Each costs about 10–19k on the Market.

### Reading the hunting ground tables
- **Hunting ground:** the name links to the entrance on the tibiamaps.io map. ✓ means the hunting ground was tested inside that band (the notes give the level and the XP/h measured).
- **Prep and Hunt:** the comfort grades, 0 to 3.
- **XP/h and Loot/h:** estimated experience and gold per hour for the typical sorcerer at the band's first and last level (~95k–185k), without bonuses and before paying for potions. Each band lists the hunting grounds that suit its first level, then the ones that open later in the band (their notes start with "From level X"); the second number shows how much more they give as you level up. The notes of tested hunting grounds give the real results.
- **Depot:** the nearest depot, how many squares you walk from it to the entrance, and about how long that takes. For islands and hidden areas it lists each step: the walk to the boat (or teleport), the trip, and the walk from where you arrive. A short trip makes a hunting ground more comfortable: more of your time goes to hunting, refilling and selling are quick, and leaving when things go wrong is easy. A mount makes every trip a bit faster: it adds 10 speed, about 5–8% faster walking. The easiest one is the Reliable Ram from Targuna's Burning Heat quest; riding a mount needs a premium account.
- **Notes:** test results, how to get access, **Watch** (what makes the hunting ground less comfortable) and **Sells** (its most valuable drop).

Under the gear line, **Unlock first** lists the quests the band's hunting grounds need (do them once, and those hunting grounds open up), and **Worth doing** points out the rare quest whose experience is worth the trip.

Each band's title also shows roughly how long it takes to reach the next band: the experience between the two levels, divided level by level by the average XP/h of its **Good for XP** list at that level. It counts raw experience plus the low level bonus (every character below level 50 gets extra experience: almost double at level 8, about 60% more at level 20, and none from level 50 on), so the stamina bonus, XP boosts and prey make it faster.

## Section 1a: Invisibility
From level 35 you can cast **`utana vid`** (440 mana), which makes you invisible for 3 minutes and 20 seconds. Many monsters can't see invisible players, so they simply ignore you. Players report that these monsters don't attack you or call helpers even while you're hitting them. Cyclops, forest furies, gloom wolves, amazons and barbarians are some of them. Stronger monsters, like dragons and dwarf guards, can see invisible players and attack you as usual.

Before level 35, a **Stealth Ring** does the same for 10 minutes. It costs about 800–1,500 gold on the Market, depending on the world.

It has its own section because it's one more thing to watch: you have to recast it before it runs out. It changes less for a sorcerer than the ring does for a knight, because kiting already keeps most melee hits away, but it can turn a hunting ground full of spear throwers or spell casters into an easy one. You can use it on top of any later section.

### Using it
1. **Cast `utana vid` before you walk into the spawn.** Keep an eye on the timer and recast before it runs out. If it ends in the middle of a group, they all attack you at once.
2. **Walk to the monsters.** Monsters that can't see you won't follow you, so you can't gather a group. Walk up to them and attack. Waves still hit every monster in front of you.
3. **Try one monster first** the first time, to be sure it really ignores you.
4. You can't log out right away while these monsters are on your screen, even while invisible.

### What it changes
- It helps most where the common monsters hit from range or cast spells but can't see invisible players.
- Monsters that can't see you never move, so you lose time walking from one to the next. The tables count that only roughly.
- **Above level 100** little changes: most monsters worth hunting there can see invisible players.

### Easy quests with invisibility
Several old item quests are guarded mostly by monsters that can't see you, so invisibility turns them into a walk. They give gear and gold, not experience. Before level 35, use a Stealth Ring:

|---|---|---|---|

| [Noble Armor Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Noble_Armor_Quest) | 35+ | Noble Armor and Crown Helmet | Ghouls |

## Section 2: Imbuements
An **imbuement** is an enchantment you add to a piece of gear for 20 hours of fighting. For a comfortable sorcerer, two matter far more than any other: **Vampirism** (life leech) and **Void** (mana leech), which turn part of the damage you deal into health and mana. You drink fewer potions and can fight bigger groups safely. This section's kit is **Basic Vampirism on your armor and Basic Void on your helmet**.

Leech gives back a share of your damage, so it pays off only once you deal a lot of it, around level 40 to 60 for a sorcerer, where mana leech matters most because every attack costs mana. That's why the Section 2 tables start at level 50.

### Setting it up
1. **Unlock imbuing** with the first part of the [Forgotten Knowledge Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Forgotten_Knowledge_Quest/Spoiler): in the Halls of Hope, just north of Thais, give 5 Heavy Old Tomes to Albinius (say "temple", then "yes"). Tomes cost about 250 gold on the Market, and orcs, trolls, skeletons, ghouls and cyclops drop them.
2. **Armor:** at an Imbuing Shrine in any big city, add Basic Vampirism with 25 Vampire Teeth and a 7,500 gold fee.
3. **Helmet:** use a Basic Void scroll from the Market. Scrolls work anywhere, without the quest or a shrine, and for Void the scroll is the cheaper way.

The kit costs about 74k gold per 20 hours of fighting, around 3.7k per hour. Costs, using Market prices from November 2026:

| Imbuement | Cheapest way, per 20 h | Per hour |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Vampirism | shrine ~19k | ~0.9k |
| Basic Void | scroll ~55k | ~2.8k |
| Basic Strike | scroll ~35k | ~1.7k |
| Intricate Vampirism | shrine ~284k | ~14.2k |
| Intricate Void | shrine ~265k | ~13.2k |

### Good to know
- The 20 hours count down only while you fight. Removing an imbuement costs 15,000 gold, so let it run out.
- An item has 0 to 3 slots and holds one imbuement of each kind. Legs can't be imbued. Wands can be imbued too.
- Worth less for the gold, so later: **Strike** (critical hits, +1% to +4.5% damage), **Epiphany** (magic level, about +1% damage per point), and **elemental protection** (3% to 15% less damage from one element).
- You can farm the materials: Vampire Teeth from vampires (Edron Vampire Crypt), Rope Belts for Void from cultists (Cult Cave, Liberty Bay), and Bloody Pincers for Intricate Vampirism from blood crabs (Laguna Islands).

### Reading the Section 2 tables
These tables grade the hunting grounds with the kit and list the 3 best for XP and the 3 best for gold per band. They only take hunting grounds where this lowers Prep and Hunt by 2 or more in total. The grades show the change, for example "2 → **0**". The XP and loot numbers are the Section 1 estimates, so treat them as a minimum: with leech you should do better, but by how much isn't tested yet.

Leech helps most against steady damage. It doesn't help against a monster that can take half your health in one hit, against monsters that call helpers or turn invisible, or against elemental damage, so those hunting grounds keep their grades.

## Section 3: Charms and prey
Charms and prey give you bonuses against specific monsters. They cost little or nothing once unlocked, but you have to choose and switch them as you change hunting grounds.

### Prey
Open it with Ctrl+Y (or right-click your character and choose "Prey System"). Each prey slot offers a list of 9 creatures. You pick one, and you get a bonus against that creature for 2 hours of hunting.
- **Slots:** 2, and a third one can be bought in the Store.
- **Bonuses:** each one has 10 steps. **Damage reduction** cuts the damage that creature does to you by 12% to 30%. **Bonus experience** gives 13% to 40% more experience from it. **Damage boost** (7% to 25%) and **improved loot** (13% to 40%) also exist.
- **New list:** free every 20 hours, or for 150 gold per level of your character.
- **Prey Wildcards** let you improve the bonus or pick the creature you want. You get 3 each week from the daily reward, and they're also sold in the Store.

The comfort setup uses both premium slots:
1. **Damage reduction** on the monster that hits you hardest.
2. **Bonus experience** (or improved loot, at a money spot) on the monster that gives you the most experience.

The tables below assume about 20% damage reduction and 25% more experience, which is what you'll usually have after a wildcard or two.

### Charms
Every creature has a **Bestiary** entry. Killing enough of that creature completes the entry in 3 stages and gives **Charm Points**, more for tougher creatures. You spend the points to unlock charms, and then place each charm on one creature with a completed entry; minor charms (below) need only the second stage. You can use 6 charms at once, and moving a charm to another creature costs 100 gold per level of your character.

The best charms for comfort:
- **Dodge** (240 points): a 5% chance to take no damage from that creature's attack (10% after an upgrade for 360 more). Put it on the monster that hurts you most.
- **Wound** (240 points): each of your attacks has a 5% chance to hit for 5% of the creature's maximum health, roughly 1–2% more damage. If the creature is weak to an element, the matching charm is better (Freeze, Zap, Enflame, Poison, Curse or Divine Wrath).
- **Minor charms**, paid with Minor Charm Echoes instead of points: Vampiric Embrace (more life leech, if your gear already leeches), Gut (6% more creature products), Bless (lose 6% less when that creature kills you) and Scavenge (a 60–120% better chance to skin or dust that creature, see Loot in Section 1). You get 50 echoes for unlocking a major charm and 100 and 200 more for its two upgrades, plus 100 at promotion; a minor charm costs 100 echoes.
- **Low Blow** and **Savage Blow** (800 points each) add critical hits. They only pay off once you have a Strike imbuement.

Charms change your average damage, not the worst hit, so they don't change the grades in the tables.

## Section 4: Wheel of Destiny and gems
From level 51, after promotion and with a premium account, you get **1 point per level** to spend on the Wheel of Destiny, a big circle of permanent perks: 40 points at level 90, 84 at 134 and 150 at 200. You can move your points around for free in any temple.

### How the Wheel works
- You start in the middle and fill **slices** with points. Each point gives a small **dedication** bonus: hit points, mana, capacity or mitigation (each point reduces damage taken a little).
- A full slice unlocks a **conviction perk**. The best one for comfort is **Enhanced Magic Shield**: your shield absorbs 8 × magic level + 8.6 × level + 300 instead of 7 × magic level + 7.6 × level + 300. Others improve your spells, such as a bigger area for `exevo vis hur`.
- With 250 points in one quarter of the Wheel (around level 300 and up) you reach the big **revelation perks**, such as Beam Mastery (beams also hit the squares next to them) and Lord of Destruction (stronger elemental stances).

A good comfort plan: Enhanced Magic Shield first. TibiaPal has a planner and ready-made sorcerer builds at tibiapal.com/wheels.

### Gems
Later, the Wheel also takes **gems**, which add extra bonuses such as more HP or elemental resistance. Lesser gems drop from influenced monsters (stronger versions of normal monsters that appear now and then), bigger ones from fiendish monsters and bosses. Revealing a gem's bonuses costs gold: 125k for a lesser gem, 1 million for a regular one and 6 million for a greater one. That's late-game spending, so gems aren't in the tables.

### Reading the Section 4 tables
These tables assume half your points go into hit-point slices, a rough estimate of about 0.5 extra HP per level above 50. That's little, so few hunting grounds change here: the Wheel helps a sorcerer mostly through Enhanced Magic Shield and stronger spells, which the tables don't count. They only take hunting grounds where this lowers Prep and Hunt by 2 or more in total.

## Section 5: Weapon proficiency
Every monster you kill with a wand equipped builds up that wand's **proficiency**. Each proficiency level unlocks a choice of perks for that wand, such as more damage, more critical hits or more leech. You pick one perk per level. You can pick anywhere, but changing a perk needs a protection zone.

Progress belongs to each wand, so switching wands often wastes it. Pick one main wand per level band and stay with it. Tougher creatures give more progress per kill. The first levels come within a few hours of hunting; the last ones take hundreds of hours, so they're for a wand you'll keep for a long time.

Each wand has its own perks, and from proficiency level 3 you can swap one of them for another perk (250 Dust). The wand's TibiaWiki page and the planner at tibiapal.com/weapon-proficiency list them.

## Section 6: The Forge and the full Master Sorcerer
The last step is the **Exaltation Forge** in the Adventurers' Guild. It raises an item's **tier**, which adds a small chance-based effect:

| Item | Effect | Tier 1 | Tier 3 | Tier 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weapon | Onslaught: chance of a hit with 60% extra damage | +0.3% damage | +1% damage | +5.4% damage |
| Armor | Ruse: chance to dodge an attack | 0.5% | 1.6% | 7.5% |
| Helmet | Momentum: chance to cut 2 seconds off your spell cooldowns | 2% every 2 s | 6% | 24% |
| Legs | Transcendence: chance to become an avatar for 7 seconds | very rare | rare | about 2% |
| Boots | Amplification: makes the other effects trigger more often | +2.5% | +9% | +57% |

**How it works.** You **fuse** two identical items of the same tier, paying gold and Dust, and get one item of the next tier. It works half the time (65% with an Exalted Core). When it fails, one of the two items loses a tier, or is destroyed if it was tier 0, and the other stays as it was; an Exalted Core makes that loss happen only half the time. So fusing your weapon with a second copy never costs more than the copy. Dust comes from influenced monsters and can't be traded; Exalted Cores are made from it.

**How far an item can go** depends on its classification, from 1 to 4. Class 1 items stop at tier 1, class 2 at tier 2, class 3 at tier 3, and class 4 items reach tier 10.

**What it costs.** Going from tier 0 to 1 costs 25k gold for a class 1 item, 750k for class 2, 4 million for class 3 and 8 million for class 4. The next tiers of a class 4 item cost 20 million, then 40 million, 65 million and more.

**Without risk.** Three Forge actions always work:
- **Transfer** moves a tier from one item to a tier 0 item of the same slot and classification, which gets the tier minus one; the item it comes from is destroyed. A tier 2 transfer (landing as tier 1) costs 100 Dust, 1 Exalted Core and 5 million gold for class 2, 10 million for class 3 or 20 million for class 4. Use it to carry your old weapon's tier to a new one, or to put the tier of a tiered item from the Market on the item you want.
- **Convergence Fusion** (class 4 only) fuses two different items of the same slot and tier, and always works: 130 Dust and 55 million gold for tier 1, against 8 million for a normal fusion.
- **Convergence Transfer** (class 4 only) moves a tier without losing one, even to another slot: 160 Dust and 65 million gold for tier 1.

The Forge also turns Dust into Slivers (60 Dust for 3) and Slivers into Exalted Cores (50 each), and raises how much Dust you can carry. Slivers and Exalted Cores sell on the Market, so Dust you don't need is gold. Items must have no imbuement to go into the Forge, so use it when one runs out.

**For comfort,** the effect that matters most is Ruse on your armor. At the tiers you can afford early (1 to 3), it's only a 0.5–1.6% dodge chance for millions of gold. It's often cheaper to buy an already-tiered item on the Market than to forge one, and to move a tier you already own, use Transfer, not Fusion. Leave the Forge for when everything else is done.

With imbuements, charms, prey, the Wheel and gems, weapon proficiency and tiered gear, you have what players call a **full setup**.

## Appendix: how the numbers are made
Nobody publishes hunting numbers for sorcerers without a full setup, so the XP, loot and grades in the sorcerer tables are calculated the same way as in the knight guide, with a sorcerer's spells, HP and gear. Here is how, in plain terms.

1. **You.** The typical sorcerer of each band: its level, magic level and the band's gear. Spells and heals use their base power on TibiaWiki (strike 45, fire wave 40, energy wave 150) times your Magic Power, (level + 4 × magic level) ÷ 100; the same formula matches the knights' real `exori` and `exura ico`. The wand adds its own shots and gives a little mana back. Your spellbook blocks a little; your armor and protections stop the rest.
2. **The monsters.** HP, experience and loot come from TibiaWiki; armor, weaknesses, healing, attacks and the helpers they call come from the Canary open-source server.
3. **The hunting ground.** Where each monster appears comes from Canary's game map: which ones come together in one pull, how far you walk between them, and how fast they come back.
4. **One fight at a time.** For each pull: how long it takes to kill (each spell after the monster's weakness to its element, so fire waves do nothing to dragons; strikes use the element the monster takes most from among fire, energy and death; on 2 or more monsters, waves hit up to 4 with `exevo flam hur` and 6 with `exevo vis hur`, with strikes in between; the wand's shots), how much they hurt you (after your armor, spellbook and protections; melee hits count half, because you kite) and how you heal (`exura`, `exura gran` from level 20, potions in the scary moments). Walking and looting fill the time between pulls.
5. **Putting it together.** Kills per hour, up to what the respawn allows, give XP/h and loot/h. Each drop is valued at whichever is higher: the shop price or the Market price (the middle price on three big worlds, November 2026); drops rarer than once every 3 hours are left out. The healing gives the grades; Mana Potions for spells and healing (after what regeneration and the wand give back) and health potions give the supplies.

Because it works fight by fight, a hunting ground's numbers grow with your level the way they really do: harder hits, faster walking and more HP, until the hunting ground runs out of monsters.

**How far to trust them:**
- Nothing here is tested with a sorcerer yet. The extra time each fight takes (moving around, looting, a few more seconds below level 50) is borrowed from 17 knight hunts.
- Where a knight test measured a hunting ground's loot, the sorcerer numbers use it too: what a monster drops doesn't depend on who kills it.
- Kiting is counted simply (half the melee damage). Fast monsters, monsters that slow you and tight caves make it harder than that.
- Magic Shield, runes and the bigger spells aren't counted; they make hunting safer and faster than the tables say.
- Supplies are a rough estimate.
- It doesn't know about other players in the same hunting ground, or about areas that change every day (Poacher Caves, Forsaken Mine).
- If a hunting ground's money comes from one single item (like Gloom Wolf Fur), hunting it a lot can push that item's Market price down.
- Targuna is newer than Canary's map, so its monsters and spawns come from another open-source server. Their attacks hit more often than the knight tests there showed, so Targuna's grades are on the harsh side.
- The few hunting grounds missing from both maps (like the Lost Dwarves in the Forsaken Mine) use the older, simpler estimate.

Use the numbers to compare hunting grounds with each other, not as promises.
