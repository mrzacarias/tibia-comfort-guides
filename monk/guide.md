# Monk hunting guide by comfort (Tibia, Nov 2026)

This guide is for anyone playing a monk alone, including players who are brand new to Tibia. It answers one question at every level: **where can I hunt without stress, and still make good experience and gold?**

Everything here describes Tibia as it is in November 2026, after the big vocation changes of June 2026. Older guides and videos may give different advice, because spells, damage and potions changed a lot.

**The numbers in this guide are untested estimates.** They come from the same model as the knight guide, changed for a monk's HP, attacks and way of fighting. Real hunts will correct them over time.

## How to use this guide
The game has many systems that make a monk stronger: imbuements, charms, the Wheel of Destiny and more. Each one helps, but each one is also something new to learn, buy or keep track of. This guide adds them **one at a time**, in sections:

- **Section 1, Basic mechanics:** just your fist weapon, the builder and spender attacks, the basic healing spells and potions. Full hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1a, Stealth Ring:** a ring that hides you from many monsters. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1b, More spells:** virtues, the Way of the Monk shrines, and bigger heals and attacks. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 1c, Skinning and dusting:** extra loot from dragon, behemoth, vampire and demon corpses, with a knife or a stake. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 2, Imbuements:** life and mana leech on your gear. Full hunting ground lists.
- **Section 3, Charms and prey:** bonuses against the monsters you hunt. Hunting ground lists.
- **Section 4, Wheel of Destiny and gems:** permanent perks from your level. Hunting ground lists.
- **Sections 5 and 6, Weapon proficiency and the Forge:** the last steps to a full setup.

Start in Section 1. Find your level in the hunting ground tables and pick a hunting ground near the top of the list: those are the most comfortable ones. When hunting there feels easy, move on to the next section and see which new hunting grounds open up.

Playing without premium? Section 1 has a part on free accounts, with the best free hunting grounds for each band.

If you test a hunting ground, write down how it went in `spot_tests.md`. Tested hunting grounds will be marked with ✓ and use real numbers instead of estimates.

## Words you'll see
- **HP and mana:** your health and the energy you spend on spells. A monk has about 185 + 10 × (level − 8) HP: 605 at level 50, 1,445 at level 134. That's less than a knight, and a monk fights up close too, so HP matters a lot.
- **Fist fighting:** your skill with fist weapons. It decides how hard you hit.
- **Spawn:** the monsters that live in a hunting ground and come back after you kill them.
- **Box, boxing:** letting several monsters surround you and hitting them all at once with an area attack. Bigger boxes kill faster but hurt more.
- **Harmony:** a monk's combo points. **Builder** attacks add one (up to 5); **spender** attacks use them all to hit much harder.
- **Serene:** a monk fighting alone is always serene, which doubles the effect of virtues.
- **`exura`, `exura gran`:** the basic healing spells.
- **XP/h, loot/h:** experience and gold (the value of what monsters drop) per hour of hunting.
- **Depot:** your storage in each city. It's also where the Market is.
- **Market:** where players buy and sell items. Many monster drops sell there for far more than shops pay.
- **Band:** a level range in the hunting ground tables, such as 40–50.

## Comfort: the idea behind this guide
Some hunting grounds ask almost nothing of you: you walk in, attack, and never need to heal. Others ask a lot: you have to watch your health all the time, carry many potions, avoid certain rooms, or bring special gear. This guide calls that difference **comfort**.

Comfort depends on your level. A hunting ground that is dangerous at level 50 can be relaxing at level 130, because you have more HP and hit harder.

Every hunting ground gets two grades from **0 (most comfortable) to 3 (least comfortable)**:
- **Hunt:** how much attention the hunt itself needs.
- **Prep:** how much you need to bring or buy before you go.

| Grade | Hunt (during the hunt) | Prep (before the hunt) |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | You just attack. No healing needed. | Nothing special besides the mana potions for your spells. |
| **1** | You heal or drink a potion now and then. | A few cheap potions. |
| **2** | You box monsters with your area attacks and drink potions steadily. | Many potions, or a specific piece of gear. |
| **3** | You need special tactics: invisible monsters, monsters that call helpers, rooms to avoid. | Something from a later section of this guide. |

The best hunting grounds are **Prep 0, Hunt 0** ("P0 H0" in short). That's the dream: no worries, just hunting.

A monk drinks mana potions for spells, so those don't count against Prep. Their cost is counted, though: a hunting ground is only listed if its loot pays for them.

There's a third kind of comfort, **knowledge**: how much of the game you need to understand. That's what the sections are for. Each section has a **kit**, the things everyone in that section uses. In Section 2, for example, everyone has the two leech imbuements, so they don't count against a hunting ground's Prep grade.

### How a hunting ground is graded
Until hunting grounds are tested with a monk, every grade comes from the monsters, using data from TibiaWiki. The main question is: **how hard can the hunting ground's strongest common monster hit you in a single turn, compared with your total HP?** "Common" means at least 1 in 10 of the monsters there. The check is done at the lowest level of each band.

For example, at level 50 you have about 605 HP. A monster that can hit 180 in one turn takes about 30% of your health, which makes the hunting ground Hunt 2.

| A common monster can hit you for… | Hunt | Prep |
|---|---|---|
| less than 15% of your HP | 0 | 0 |
| 15% to 30% | 1 | 1 |
| 30% to 50% | 2 | 2 |
| half your HP or more | 3 | 3 |

A few other things make a hunting ground less comfortable:
- **Elemental damage.** Fire, ice, energy, earth and death damage go straight through normal armor. If most of a hunting ground's damage is elemental, its Hunt grade goes up one step, and you'll usually want special gear (Prep 2).
- **Invisible monsters.** You can't see them to attack them, so the hunting ground is Hunt 3.
- **Monsters that call helpers** ("summoners"), or a rare monster that can take half your HP in one turn, make the hunting ground at least Hunt 2.
- **Special gear.** If the hunting ground needs a specific item, like a Dwarven Ring, Prep is 2.

A monk fights up close like a knight, so every hit counts in full.

Some hunting grounds are left out entirely: those full of summoners, or with many monsters that hurt you but give no experience.

### Which hunting grounds are listed
A hunting ground appears in every band from its recommended level upwards, so the same hunting ground shows up in several bands. Its grades and numbers get better as your level goes up.

Each band shows only its **5 best hunting grounds**. Hunting grounds that aren't worth your time are dropped first: a hunting ground's experience **or** its loot must be at least three quarters of the third-best hunting ground in that band, and its loot must cover the potions it costs. The rest are sorted by comfort (Prep first, then Hunt), then by loot, and the top 5 are listed.

Each hunting ground is tagged as an **XP** hunting ground (good for levelling), a **money** hunting ground (good for gold), or **XP + money**.

### The character the numbers assume
The XP and loot numbers are estimates for a typical monk: someone whose fist fighting is a little below the usual for their level (30 at level 8, 63 at 30, 96 at 90, 114 at 200), with the best simple fist weapon for that level. A new character only reaches those skills by training offline from the start (see Section 1). Each band shows that skill and weapon. If yours are better, you'll do better than the tables.

The estimates also assume that an area attack hits about 2.5 monsters on average, and that you fight at the same pace as the knight guide's tests: walking, gathering monsters and looting take most of the time, not the attacking itself.

## Section 1: Basic mechanics
In this section you use only the basics: your fist weapon, the builder and spender attacks described below, the `exura` healing spells, potions and normal gear. Everything else comes later.

### How to fight
A monk fights up close, like a knight, but has less HP and no shield, because fist weapons need both hands. So pick fights carefully and heal early.

**Harmony** is what makes a monk different. **Builder** attacks each add one harmony point, up to 5. A **spender** attack uses all your harmony and hits harder for each point: 7% more with one point, 28% with three, 112% with all five. Every time you build or spend harmony, you also heal a little (virtue healing).

**Your attacks:**
- **Builders:** `exori infir pug` (Swift Jab, any level, 3 mana) and `exori pug` (Double Jab, level 14, 30 mana) hit the monster next to you. From level 35, `exori mas pug` (Flurry of Blows, 110 mana, premium account) hits every monster in front of you and to your sides, and builds harmony too.
- **Spenders:** `exori infir nia` (Tiger Clash, any level, 18 mana), and from level 18 the stronger `exori nia` (premium), hit the monster next to you. From level 60, `exori mas nia` (Sweeping Takedown, 195 mana, premium) hits a group.

All attack spells share a 2-second cooldown. A simple rhythm: three builders, then a spender, while your weapon keeps hitting. From level 35: Flurry of Blows, Double Jab, Flurry of Blows, then a spender.

**Boxing:** from level 35, let a few monsters surround you and use your area attacks. Start with two or three monsters and add one more at a time as long as your HP stays above about 60%.

**Healing:** `exura` (level 8, 20 mana) heals a little, and `exura gran` (level 20, 70 mana) heals more. Virtue healing helps on top.

**Potions:** all potions share one short cooldown (about 1 second), so you can't drink a health and a mana potion at the same moment. Mana Potions are the cheapest per point of mana, and the tables count them as supplies. Stronger mana potions unlock at level 50 (Strong), 80 (Great) and 100 (Superior). For health you get the Strong Health Potion at level 50; the bigger health potions are for knights only.

**Spirit potions** (monks and paladins only) restore health and mana in one drink, so one cooldown covers both: the Great Spirit Potion (level 80, 254 gold, about 300 health and 150 mana) and the Ultimate Spirit Potion (level 130, 488 gold, about 500 health and 200 mana). They cost more than buying the same health and mana separately, but when both run low at once, one drink fixes both. The Ultimate is also the biggest heal a monk can drink.

**Stay safe:** always know your way out, and leave when your HP falls to about half. Don't wait until it's almost empty.

### Before you hunt
**Stamina** is how long your character can hunt at full strength. It goes up to 42 hours. With a premium account, the first 3 hours from full stamina give **50% more experience**, so start your best hunts right after a long rest. Below 14 hours you only get half the experience and no loot, so stop and rest. Stamina comes back while you are logged out: about 1 minute for every 3 minutes offline, and half as fast for the 3 bonus hours.

**Blessings** are bought from NPCs in the cities and protect you when you die. With the 5 regular blessings you lose about 70% less experience and skill, and you keep all your items. Together they cost about (level − 20) × 1,000 gold up to level 120, so about 70k at level 90, and about 105k at level 134. They're worth it.

**Food:** eat before and during the hunt. Food makes you regenerate health and mana, up to 20 minutes at a time, so eat until the game says you're full and eat again now and then. Any food works; ham and brown mushrooms are cheap and common.

**Daily reward:** once a day, open the Reward Wall (the button under your inventory, or the shrine in any temple or depot) and take your reward. Over each 7 days a premium account gets 40 potions or runes of your choice, 3 Prey Wildcards, two training weapons, a temple teleport scroll, a gold converter (it turns 100 gold coins into 1 platinum coin, saving capacity) and a 30-minute XP boost; free accounts get about half. Taking it every day also builds a streak: in temples, depots and houses you then regenerate faster, and from the fourth day premium accounts also get stamina back there.

### Quick Loot
Looting every corpse by hand is slow, and it's easy to miss one. **Quick Loot** does it in one click: with the regular controls, Shift + right click on a corpse sends everything inside to your backpack, even from a pile of up to 30 corpses on one square. You can change the click in the Options.
- **One key for everything around you:** **Quick Loot Nearby Corpses** (since July 2024) loots your square and the 8 around it at once. In the Options, under Hotkeys, put it on a key right next to your attack and healing keys, like ` (left of 1). Then you loot between attacks and heals without reaching for the mouse.
- **Skip the junk:** right-click an item and choose "Add to Loot List". In **Skipped Loot** mode, Quick Loot picks up everything except the items on the list, so you never leave something valuable behind by mistake. Add cheap, heavy items as you meet them.
- **Sort it (premium):** you can give each backpack a category, like gold, creature products or potions, and Quick Loot puts each item in the right one. Selling gets much faster.

Free accounts can use Quick Loot too; only the sorting needs premium.

### Selling your loot
Many things monsters drop sell on the Market for much more than any shop pays. A Stone Wing, for example, sells for about 2.4k on the Market but only 120 at a shop. The notes for each hunting ground name its most valuable drop.

The in-game Hunt Analyser often shows less loot than you really made, because it values items at shop prices. In one tested hour it showed 10.6k of loot; selling everything on the Market brought in 106k.

Prices also differ between game worlds, sometimes by three times, so check your own Market before choosing a hunting ground for its loot.

### The daily boosted creature
Every day at server save, one creature becomes the **boosted creature** until the next server save. That creature gives **twice the experience and twice the loot, and it respawns twice as fast**. You can see it on the client's login screen and at the top right of tibia.com.

Check it before you pick a hunting ground. If today's creature lives in a hunting ground for your level, hunt there today: double experience and loot usually beats everything else in your band. The boost also adds to prey (Section 3) and to experience events. The same creature can't be boosted again for 30 days.

### Hunting tasks (the Task Board)
The **Task Board** in the game client gives you two kinds of tasks. Both pay you for hunting you would do anyway, so take them from the start.

**Bounty tasks** ask you to kill a number of one creature, and pay experience and **Bounty Points**. Pick creatures from a hunting ground you already like.

| Task | Kills | Bounty Points |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 50–100 | 3 |
| Adept | 100–200 | 7 |
| Expert | 200–400 | 16 |
| Master | 300–600 | 27 |

Sometimes a task comes up **Silver** (twice the experience and points) or **Gold** (four times). You spend the points on the **Bounty Talisman**, which you buy from NPCs for 5,000 gold and wear in the extra slot. Its upgrades only work against your current bounty creature: more damage against it (up to 50%), life leech (up to 50%), more loot (up to 50%) and a chance of double Bestiary progress (up to 100%). Points also pay for new task choices and for marking creatures you want or don't want to see.

**Weekly tasks** change every Monday: 6 kill tasks and 6 delivery tasks (bring certain items). Up to level 82, each one gives **half a level of experience**, so finishing all 12 is worth about 6 levels a week. After level 82 each gives about 2,000 × your level. At the end of the week you also get Hunting Task Points for outfits, mounts and other rewards in the Task Board shop.

The hunting ground numbers in this guide don't count the boosted creature, tasks or the talisman, so all of them are a bonus on top.

### Train your skills while you're offline
Your fist fighting decides how hard you hit, and at low levels it matters more than your level. You can train it for free while you're logged out:

1. Go to a **Training Statue** that offers **Fist Fighting + Shielding**. TibiaWiki lists the ones in the Adventurers' Guild and the Blue Valley temple; the statue on Targuna also lets you pick your skill. Later, a bed in a house works the same way.
2. Use it, pick Fist Fighting, and log out. Training starts after 10 minutes offline.
3. You can train up to **12 hours in a row**. The time comes back one second for every second you spend online, or offline without training.

It's worth doing **right away, from level 8**. A knight with a similar starting skill gains about 25 skill points in the first 12 hours, which more than doubles the damage you deal; a monk should gain about the same. It costs nothing, so make it a habit: whenever you log out, use a training statue.

A few more things:
- Hunting trains your skill too, about twice as fast as offline training, as long as you keep attacking.
- The daily reward sometimes gives free training weapons. Use them on the Exercise Dummy in any training school for a little extra skill.
- Free accounts train more slowly offline and stop at skill 50.

### Promotion at level 20
Until now you are a Monk. At **level 20**, with a premium account, you can buy your **promotion** for 20,000 gold and become an **Exalted Monk**. The easiest place is King Tibianus III in Thais's castle; Queen Eloise in Carlin, Emperor Kruzak in Kazordoon, Grand Vizier Ishebad in Ankrahmun, Emperor Rehal in Beregar and Rotem Valos at the Adventurers' Guild sell it too.

It's worth buying as soon as you have the gold:
- You lose 30% less experience and skill when you die.
- You regenerate faster: 1 HP every 5 seconds instead of every 7, and 2 mana every 3 seconds instead of every 4.
- You can learn promotion-only spells, and from level 51 you get Wheel of Destiny points (Section 4).
- You get 100 Minor Charm Echoes for minor charms (Section 3).

The promotion is permanent, but it pauses while your premium time is over.

### Playing on a free account
You can start Tibia without paying, but a free account has real limits. The ones that matter most for a monk:
- **No area attacks.** Flurry of Blows, Sweeping Takedown and the stronger Tiger Clash are premium, so a free monk fights with the jabs, Tiger Clash and the `exura` spells. Big groups stay slow, so fight one or two monsters at a time.
- **No promotion,** so you stay a Monk, and no Wheel of Destiny or virtues.
- **Six cities only:** Thais, Carlin, Venore, Ab'Dendriel, Kazordoon and Yalahar. Boats, magic carpets and steamships to premium places are closed, so most islands are out. Targuna and its three hunting grounds are free.
- **No stamina bonus** (the 50% extra experience in the first 3 hours).
- **Offline training** is slower and stops at skill 50.
- **Fewer helpers later:** 1 prey slot instead of 2, 2 charms instead of 6, and only Basic imbuements (for Intricate ones you need an Imbuement Scroll from the Market).
- **Many quests are premium.** The quest table above says which ones are free.

### Reading the hunting ground tables
- **Hunting ground:** the name links to the entrance on the tibiamaps.io map. ✓ means the hunting ground was tested at that level.
- **Prep and Hunt:** the comfort grades, 0 to 3.
- **XP/h and Loot/h:** estimated experience and gold per hour for the typical monk at the lowest level of the band, without bonuses and before paying for potions. Tested hunting grounds show real results.
- **Depot:** the nearest depot, how many squares you walk from it to the entrance, and about how long that takes. For islands and hidden areas it lists each step: the walk to the boat (or teleport), the trip, and the walk from where you arrive. A short trip makes a hunting ground more comfortable: more of your time goes to hunting, refilling and selling are quick, and leaving when things go wrong is easy. A mount makes every trip a bit faster: it adds 10 speed, about 5–8% faster walking. The easiest one is the Reliable Ram from Targuna's Burning Heat quest (see Easy quests before level 20); riding a mount needs a premium account.
- **Notes:** the tag (XP, money or both), test results, how to get access, **Watch** (what makes the hunting ground less comfortable) and **Sells** (its most valuable drop).

Under the gear line, **Unlock first** lists the quests the band's hunting grounds need (do them once, and those hunting grounds open up), and **Worth doing** points out the rare quest whose experience is worth the trip.

Each band's title also shows roughly how long it takes to reach the next band: the experience between the two levels, divided by the average XP/h of the hunting grounds listed. It counts raw experience only, so the stamina bonus, XP boosts and prey make it faster.

## Section 1a: Stealth Ring
A **Stealth Ring** makes you invisible for 10 minutes. Many monsters can't see invisible players, so they simply ignore you. Players report that these monsters don't attack you or call helpers even while you're hitting them. You can kill them without taking any damage. Cyclops, forest furies, gloom wolves, amazons and barbarians are some of them. Stronger monsters, like dragons and dwarf guards, can see invisible players and attack you as usual.

The ring has its own section because it asks for more preparation: you have to buy rings and swap them every 10 minutes. In return, several better-paying hunting grounds become comfortable much earlier, especially between levels 20 and 90. You can use it on top of any later section.

### Using it
1. **Buy rings on the Market.** They cost about 800–1,500 gold each, depending on the world (around 1,000 is typical). You use 6 per hour, so about 6k gold per hour. The NPC Haroun in Ankrahmun sells them for 5,000 each, which is much more expensive.
2. **Put one on before you walk into the spawn**, and keep the next one ready on a hotkey. Swap when the current ring is almost used up. If it runs out in the middle of a group, they all attack you at once.
3. **Walk to the monsters.** Monsters that can't see you won't follow you, so you can't gather a group. Walk up to them and attack; your area attacks still hit all the monsters next to you.
4. **Try one monster first** the first time, to be sure it really ignores you.
5. You can't log out right away while these monsters are on your screen, even with the ring on.

### What it changes
- It helps most where the common monsters can't see invisible players: cyclops, forest furies, gloom wolves, amazons and barbarians, for example.
- Monsters that can't see you never move, so you lose time walking from one to the next, and you can't box them. The tables count that only roughly.
- **Above level 100** little changes: most monsters worth hunting there can see invisible players.

### Easy quests with the ring
Several old item quests are guarded mostly by monsters that can't see you, so the ring turns them into a walk. They give gear, not experience, but the gear is useful early on:

A dash ("– → 0") means the hunting ground isn't in Section 1 at all, because without the ring it's too annoying. "Can't see you" names the main monsters that will ignore you; "Still" names the strongest one that can still hit you.

The XP and loot numbers use the same killing speed as Section 1. Real results may be different: you can't gather groups, but you also never stop to heal. Loot is shown before paying for the rings.

## Section 1b: More spells and the Way of the Monk
Section 1 uses only the jabs, Tiger Clash, the area attacks and the `exura` spells on purpose: every extra spell is one more button to watch during the hunt. But a few things make hunting calmer once you're used to the basics. They all need a premium account.

### Virtues and the Way of the Monk
From level 20 you can switch on a **virtue** (210 mana), which lasts until you change it. A monk fighting alone is serene, so the effect is doubled:
- **Virtue of Sustain:** all your monk healing is 70% stronger, including virtue healing. This is the comfort pick.
- **Virtue of Justice:** 16% more fist fighting. It's the damage pick.
- **Virtue of Harmony:** spenders get a bigger bonus from harmony.

**The Way of the Monk Quest** sends you to 10 ancient shrines around the world, from Targuna and Thais to much more dangerous places (TibiaWiki suggests level 275 to finish it). Each shrine makes monsters' melee attacks deal 2% less damage to you, up to 20%, and gives more auto-attack damage. The first shrines are easy to reach, so start early.

### Bigger heals
- **`exura gran tio`** (Spirit Mend, level 80, 210 mana) heals much more than `exura gran`.

**Emergency items:** a **Stone Skin Amulet** takes 80% off physical and death damage for 5 hits, and a **Might Ring** takes 20% off all damage for 20 hits. Each costs about 10–19k on the Market, too much to wear all the time, so keep one in your backpack and put it on when a pull goes wrong.

### Bigger attacks
- **`exori amp pug`** (Mystic Repulse, level 30, 150 mana) hits one monster up to 7 squares away.
- **`exori med pug`** (Chained Penance, level 70, 180 mana) hits a monster and jumps to up to 4 more.
- **`exori gran mas pug`** (Greater Flurry of Blows, level 90, 300 mana) is a much bigger Flurry of Blows.
- **`exori mas amp pug`** (Thousand Fist Blows, level 120, 145 mana) is a builder that hits your target and the monsters around it.

**Familiar:** from level 200, `utevo gran res tio` (premium, 1,500 mana) summons a strong familiar that fights at your side for 15 minutes. You can cast it once every 30 minutes.

### Moving
- **`utani hur`** (level 14, 60 mana) makes you 30% faster for 30 seconds and removes paralysis.

## Section 1c: Skinning and dusting
Some monsters leave something extra if you use the right tool on their corpse: dragons give leather, vampires give dust. Most tries give nothing, but a few of these products sell for thousands of gold on the Market, so at the right hunting grounds they can double your loot. It costs one extra click per kill and doesn't make any hunting ground safer, so this section only lists the hunting grounds where that click pays well.

### The tools
- **Obsidian Knife**, for skinning dragons, dragon lords and behemoths. The [Obsidian Knife Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Obsidian_Knife_Quest) works on a free account and costs about 4.5k: bring a Dragon Shield to A Sweaty Cyclops in Ab'Dendriel, who turns it into a Piece of Draconian Steel, then take the steel and an Obsidian Lance to Uzgod in Kazordoon, who makes the knife. On the Market it costs about 20–35k.
- **Blessed Wooden Stake**, for dusting vampires and demons. [The Blessed Stake Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/The_Blessed_Stake_Quest) needs a premium account and takes 20–30 minutes: buy a wooden stake from Gamon in Thais for 5,000 gold, then take it to the priests of many cities for their blessing (bring about 1,100 gold for boats and carpets). On the Market it costs 50k or more.

Both tools last forever, so you only need one of each.

### Using them
1. **Put the tool on an action bar button** set to "use with crosshair", so one press lets you click a corpse.
2. **After each kill, use it on the corpse.** You have 5 minutes after the kill, and each corpse can be tried only once.
3. **Green sparkles** mean success: the product appears in the same backpack as the tool. Yellow sparks mean nothing this time.

### Which monsters are worth it

| Monster | Tool | Success | Product, Market price | Extra gold per kill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dragon | Knife | 4% | Green Dragon Leather, ~16k | ~670 |
| Behemoth | Knife | 9% | Perfect Behemoth Fang, ~3.4k | ~320 |
| Vampire, Vampire Bride, Vampire Viscount | Stake | 5% | Vampire Dust, ~5.6k | ~280 |
| Demon | Stake | 10% | Demon Dust, ~2.5k | ~240 |
| Dragon Lord | Knife | 8% | Red Dragon Leather, ~2.2k | ~170 |

- **Prices change a lot between worlds.** Green Dragon Leather sells for about 6k on some worlds and 18k on others, so check your world's Market first.
- **Luck matters.** With a 4% chance, a dragon lair gives about 4–6 leathers an hour, and some hours give none.
- **Not worth it:** minotaurs, lizards and bonebeasts can be skinned too, but their products sell for under 100 gold.
- **Later:** the Scavenge charm (Section 3) raises the success chance on one kind of monster by 60%, 90% or 120%, depending on its level. On dragons it pays off.

## Section 2: Imbuements
An **imbuement** is a temporary enchantment you add to a piece of gear. It lasts for 20 hours of fighting, then wears off and you add a new one.

For a comfortable monk, two imbuements matter far more than any other:
- **Vampirism (life leech):** part of the damage you deal comes back to you as health.
- **Void (mana leech):** part of the damage you deal comes back as mana.

Together they mean the monsters you hit pay for your healing and your spells. You drink fewer potions, worry less about your health, and can safely fight bigger groups. That's why this section's kit is **Basic Vampirism on your armor and Basic Void on your helmet**. The other imbuements help too, but they give much less for the gold.

### When to start
Imbuements have no level requirement, and most armors and helmets have slots for them.

But leech gives back a share of the damage you deal, and low-level characters don't deal much. A typical level 8 monk deals about 8k damage per hour in these estimates, so Basic leech gives back only about 0.4k health and 0.2k mana per hour. Meanwhile the kit costs about 3.7k gold per hour.

It starts paying for itself **around level 35 to 50**. By then you deal 30–60k damage per hour, Flurry of Blows costs real mana, and potions become a real expense. The Section 2 tables still start at level 8, so you can see which hunting grounds it would help.

### Setting it up
1. **Unlock imbuing** with the first part of the [Forgotten Knowledge Quest](https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Forgotten_Knowledge_Quest/Spoiler). It's easy and safe at any level: walk to the Halls of Hope, just north of Thais, and give 5 Heavy Old Tomes to the NPC Albinius (say "temple", then "yes"). Tomes cost about 250 gold each on the Market, and they also drop from orcs, trolls, skeletons, ghouls and cyclops. This unlocks the Basic level for everyone and the Intricate level for premium accounts.
2. **Armor:** go to an Imbuing Shrine in any big city and add Basic Vampirism. You need 25 Vampire Teeth (cheap on the Market) and pay a 7,500 gold fee.
3. **Helmet:** buy a Basic Void scroll on the Market and use it on your helmet. Scrolls work anywhere, without a shrine. For Basic Void the scroll is cheaper than the shrine.
4. **Try it** at a hunting ground you already know, pulling one or two more monsters than usual. Write down how it went.

If you'd rather skip the quest, Imbuement Scrolls work for any player, without the quest and without a shrine. A Basic Vampirism scroll costs about 33k on the Market, a bit more than the ~19k the shrine route costs.

The whole kit costs about 74k gold for 20 hours of fighting, around 3.7k gold per hour. That's a small part of what a decent hunt makes.

### What changes when you have leech
In these estimates a level 134 monk deals about 140k damage per hour. With the kit, that would give back about 7k health and 4k mana every hour. Area attacks give back less mana for each extra monster hit.

At hunting grounds where you never needed to heal, not much changes. At hunting grounds where you were drinking potions, you'll drink fewer and hunt more calmly. The biggest gain should come from boxing bigger groups safely, but how much that adds hasn't been tested yet.

### Good to know
- The 20 hours only count down while you're fighting.
- An item can hold one imbuement of each kind, and each item has 0 to 3 slots for them. Legs can't be imbued. Fist weapons can be imbued too.
- Removing an imbuement costs 15,000 gold, so usually you just let it run out.

### Other imbuements (later, less value for the gold)
- **Strike** (weapon) adds critical hits: about +1% damage at Basic, +2% at Intricate and +4.5% at Powerful.
- **Skill imbuements** (helmet or weapon) add fist fighting: +1, +2 or +4, which is roughly +0.7% damage per point.
- **Elemental protection** (armor) reduces one element's damage by 3%, 8% or 15%. It's useful at hunting grounds that hit mostly with one element.

### Farm your own materials
Some Section 1 hunting grounds drop imbuement materials, so you can imbue with your own loot:
- **Vampire Teeth** come from vampires, for example in the Edron Vampire Crypt.
- **Rope Belts** (for Void) come from cultists and dark monks, for example in the Cult Cave near Liberty Bay.
- **Bloody Pincers** (for Intricate Vampirism) come from blood crabs at Laguna Islands and Meriana.

## Section 3: Charms and prey
Charms and prey give you bonuses against specific monsters. They cost little or nothing once unlocked, but you have to choose and switch them as you change hunting grounds.

### Prey
Open it with Ctrl+Y (or right-click your character and choose "Prey System"). Each prey slot offers a list of 9 creatures. You pick one, and you get a bonus against that creature for 2 hours of hunting.
- **Slots:** free accounts have 1, premium accounts have 2, and a third one can be bought in the Store.
- **Bonuses:** each one has 10 steps. **Damage reduction** cuts the damage that creature does to you by 12% to 30%. **Bonus experience** gives 13% to 40% more experience from it. **Damage boost** (7% to 25%) and **improved loot** (13% to 40%) also exist.
- **New list:** free every 20 hours, or for 150 gold per level of your character.
- **Prey Wildcards** let you improve the bonus or pick the creature you want. You get some each week from the daily reward (1 for free accounts, 3 for premium), and they're also sold in the Store.

The comfort setup uses both premium slots:
1. **Damage reduction** on the monster that hits you hardest.
2. **Bonus experience** (or improved loot, at a money spot) on the monster that gives you the most experience.

The tables below assume about 20% damage reduction and 25% more experience, which is what you'll usually have after a wildcard or two.

### Charms
Every creature has a **Bestiary** entry. Killing enough of that creature completes the entry and gives **Charm Points**, more for tougher creatures. You spend the points to unlock charms, and then place each charm on one creature with a completed entry. Premium accounts can use 6 charms at once (free accounts 2), and moving a charm to another creature costs 100 gold per level of your character.

The best charms for comfort:
- **Dodge** (240 points): a 5% chance to take no damage from that creature's attack (10% after an upgrade for 360 more). Put it on the monster that hurts you most.
- **Wound** (240 points): each of your attacks has a 5% chance to hit for 5% of the creature's maximum health, roughly 1–2% more damage. If the creature is weak to an element, the matching charm is better (Freeze, Zap, Enflame, Poison, Curse or Divine Wrath).
- **Minor charms**, paid with Minor Charm Echoes instead of points: Vampiric Embrace (more life leech, if your gear already leeches), Gut (6% more creature products), Bless (lose 6% less when that creature kills you) and Scavenge (a 60–120% better chance to skin or dust that creature, see Section 1c).
- **Low Blow** and **Savage Blow** (800 points each) add critical hits. They only pay off once you have a Strike imbuement.

Charms change your average damage, not the worst hit, so they don't change the grades in the tables.

## Section 4: Wheel of Destiny and gems
From level 51, after promotion and with a premium account, you get **1 point per level** to spend on the Wheel of Destiny, a big circle of permanent perks: 40 points at level 90, 84 at 134 and 150 at 200. You can move your points around for free in any temple.

### How the Wheel works
- You start in the middle and fill **slices** with points. Each point gives a small **dedication** bonus: hit points, mana, capacity or mitigation (each point reduces damage taken a little).
- A full slice unlocks a **conviction perk**, which improves one of your spells, for example a bigger area for Flurry of Blows or a shorter cooldown for Thousand Fist Blows.
- With 250 points in one quarter of the Wheel (around level 300 and up) you reach the big **revelation perks**.

TibiaPal has a planner and ready-made monk builds at tibiapal.com/wheels.

### Gems
Later, the Wheel also takes **gems**, which add extra bonuses such as more HP or elemental resistance. Lesser gems drop from influenced monsters (stronger versions of normal monsters that appear now and then), bigger ones from fiendish monsters and bosses. Revealing a gem's bonuses costs gold: 125k for a lesser gem, 1 million for a regular one and 6 million for a greater one. That's late-game spending, so gems aren't in the tables.

## Section 5: Weapon proficiency
Every monster you kill with a weapon equipped builds up that weapon's **proficiency**. Each proficiency level unlocks a choice of perks for that weapon, such as more damage, more critical hits or more leech. You pick one perk per level. You can pick anywhere, but changing a perk needs a protection zone.

Progress belongs to each weapon, so switching weapons often wastes it. Pick one main fist weapon per level band and stay with it. Tougher creatures give more progress per kill. The first levels come within a few hours of hunting; the last ones take hundreds of hours, so they're for a weapon you'll keep for a long time.

Each weapon has its own perks, and from proficiency level 3 you can swap one of them for another perk (250 Dust). The weapon's TibiaWiki page and the planner at tibiapal.com/weapon-proficiency list them.

## Section 6: The Forge and the full Exalted Monk
The last step is the **Exaltation Forge** in the Adventurers' Guild. It raises an item's **tier**, which adds a small chance-based effect:

| Item | Effect | Tier 1 | Tier 3 | Tier 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weapon | Onslaught: chance of a hit with 60% extra damage | +0.3% damage | +1% damage | +5.4% damage |
| Armor | Ruse: chance to dodge an attack | 0.5% | 1.6% | 7.5% |
| Helmet | Momentum: chance to cut 2 seconds off your spell cooldowns | 2% every 2 s | 6% | 24% |
| Legs | Transcendence: chance to become an avatar for 7 seconds | very rare | rare | about 2% |
| Boots | Amplification: makes the other effects trigger more often | +2.5% | +9% | +57% |

**How it works.** You **fuse** two identical items of the same tier, paying gold and Dust, and get one item of the next tier. It works half the time (65% with an Exalted Core). When it fails, one of the items loses a tier, or is destroyed if it was tier 0. Dust comes from influenced monsters and can't be traded; Exalted Cores are made from it.

**How far an item can go** depends on its classification, from 1 to 4. Class 1 items stop at tier 1, class 2 at tier 2, class 3 at tier 3, and class 4 items reach tier 10.

**What it costs.** Going from tier 0 to 1 costs 25k gold for a class 1 item, 750k for class 2, 4 million for class 3 and 8 million for class 4. The next tiers of a class 4 item cost 20 million, then 40 million, 65 million and more.

**For comfort,** the effect that matters most is Ruse on your armor. At the tiers you can afford early (1 to 3), it's only a 0.5–1.6% dodge chance for millions of gold. It's often cheaper to buy an already-tiered item on the Market than to forge one. Leave the Forge for when everything else is done.

With imbuements, charms, prey, the Wheel and gems, weapon proficiency and tiered gear, you have what players call a **full setup**.

## Appendix: how the numbers are made
Nobody publishes hunting numbers for monks without a full setup, so the XP and loot numbers are calculated. Here is how, in plain terms.

1. **Monsters and loot.** For each hunting ground, TibiaWiki lists which monsters live there, how much experience and HP each one has, and what it drops and how often. Each drop is valued at whichever is higher: the shop price or the Market price (the middle price on three big worlds, November 2026). Very rare drops (less than 1 in 1,000 kills) count at shop price, so lucky jackpots don't inflate the numbers.
2. **Which monsters you meet.** The mix of monsters comes from where they appear near the hunting ground's entrance on a fan-made copy of the game map.
3. **How hard you hit.** Auto-attacks use the knight guide's formula with fist fighting and the weapon's attack. Spells use their base power from TibiaWiki times (level + 4 × fist fighting) ÷ 100, the same shape as Magic Power, with fist fighting in place of magic level (an assumption). Every 8 seconds the model casts three builders and one spender, which spends 3 harmony for 28% more damage. Area attacks are counted as hitting about 2.5 monsters.
4. **Elements.** All of it is physical damage, multiplied by how much physical damage each monster takes.
5. **How fast you kill.** There are no monk tests yet, so the pace is set equal to the knight guide's level 134 tests (about 144k damage per hour) and scaled by how hard the typical monk hits at each level. Like the knight model, it's adjusted for monster HP, capped at 550 kills per hour and at what the hunting ground can respawn.
6. **Supplies.** The spells' mana, minus about 2,400 mana per hour of regeneration, is paid with Mana Potions (56 gold for about 100 mana). Healing potions are added from the Prep grade, like in the knight guide.
7. **Grades.** As in the knight guide, but with a monk's HP. Virtue healing isn't counted, so hunts may feel easier than the grades say.

**How far to trust them:**
- Nothing here is tested with a monk yet. The pace in particular is borrowed from knight hunts.
- The estimates don't know about walking between scattered monsters, other players in the same hunting ground, or areas that change every day (Poacher Caves, Forsaken Mine).
- If a hunting ground's money comes from one single item (like Gloom Wolf Fur), hunting it a lot can push that item's Market price down.

Use the numbers to compare hunting grounds with each other, not as promises.
